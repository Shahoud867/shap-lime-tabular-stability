## Gaps Addressed (G1–G4)

- **G1 (Fixed-model, prediction-preserving stability gap in credit scoring):** The base paper (`ballegeer2025`) and recent credit-XAI studies (`lin2025shap`, `chen2024`) induce instability exclusively via model retraining or data resampling. Local explanation stability under **prediction-preserving input perturbations at a fixed trained model** remains untested on credit-scoring data for **both** SHAP and LIME across linear and tree-ensemble classifiers (`tolay2026` covers TreeSHAP on IoT data; `hasnain2026` covers software defect prediction without noise-floor controls).
- **G2 (Confounded explainer stochasticity):** Prior stability audits frequently conflate genuine model sensitivity with Monte Carlo sampling variance in stochastic explainers—specifically `PermutationExplainer` in `ballegeer2025` and random neighborhood sampling in standard LIME (`nnadi2026`, `kelodjou2024`). Neither `ballegeer2025` nor credit perturbation audits quantify an instance-level **explainer noise floor** or decompose across-refit variance into model versus explainer components.
- **G3 (Absent instance-level mechanistic explanation):** Although theoretical studies note that split crossings cause attribution discontinuities (`pala2024`, `breeden2026`) and discretisation harms LIME astuteness (`khan2024`), no study tests split-threshold and bin-edge crossings ($C_{\text{tree}}, C_{\text{bin}}$) as a quantified **mediating mechanism**—nor establishes a double dissociation between TreeSHAP and discretised LIME—against noise floors in credit scoring.
- **G4 (No structure-only, perturbation-free fragility score):** Existing instability warnings require ensemble epistemic uncertainty (`mikriukov2026`), surrogate GP/geodesic fitting (`hill2024`), or conformal calibration sets (`breeden2026`). None derives a closed-form expected-crossing fragility score $F(x)$ directly from learned tree thresholds or LIME bin edges under realistic input-noise scales without running perturbations or calibration sets.
